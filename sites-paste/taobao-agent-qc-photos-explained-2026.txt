How Taobao Agent QC Works in 2026: Quality Check Photos Explained

Buying from Taobao as an overseas shopper has one big blind spot: you cannot touch, hold, or try on the item before it travels thousands of miles. QC photos — quality check photos taken by your buying agent's warehouse — are how you see the actual product before committing to international shipping. This guide explains what those photos are, how to read each image, the exact point at which a defect gets flagged, and what QC cannot tell you.

What QC photos actually are

When a Taobao seller ships your order, it goes to your agent's warehouse in China — not to your door. On arrival, warehouse staff run a standard quality inspection: they screen all purchased goods for prohibited items, then verify the appearance details of the products, including style, quantity, color, size, model, damage, stains, and defects. During the inspection they provide 3 to 5 standard inspection photos per item, depending on the product category. Goods are repackaged after inspection, and the original packaging may not be fully restored.

The photos are the point where you, the buyer, re-enter the process. You review the images in your account before anything is consolidated or shipped internationally. Approve, and the order moves forward. Spot a problem, and you can ask for a return or exchange while the item is still in China, where domestic returns are far cheaper and faster than after delivery.

That checkpoint is the real value of a buying agent.

Free QC vs paid QC: what you get

Most agents give you some photos for free and charge for extras. The two tiers look like this, in CFans' published terms:

**Free: standard quality inspection** — Photo count: 3–5 per item (varies by product category); Covers: prohibited-item screening + visible checks of style, quantity, color, size, model, damage, stains, defects; Extra fee: none stated (default flow, no separate charge)

**Paid upgrade: HD custom photos** — Price: 1.5 RMB per photo; Delivery: within 24 hours; Use case: close-ups of a specific detail, extra angles the standard set did not cover

**Paid upgrade: video QC** — Price: 35 RMB per item; Format: 20–90 second video; Delivery: within 24 hours; Use case: items where shape, movement, or texture matters — shoes, bags, fabrics, anything a still photo flattens

Three to five photos is enough for most simple items — a phone case, a cable, a t-shirt. For anything where fine detail decides the purchase (stitching on a jacket, the clasp on a bag, the sole of a shoe), request the paid HD photos before approving shipment. At 1.5 RMB per photo, two or three targeted close-ups are the cheapest insurance in the whole order.

How long QC takes

Set expectations before you order. After you pay your agent, the purchase itself is completed within 24 hours (CFans' order response windows: within 6 hours for requests made 09:00–18:00, or processed by next day 14:00 for requests made 18:00–09:00). The domestic seller then ships to the warehouse on its own timeline — usually a few days.

The warehouse check-in QC itself takes 3–5 business days from domestic arrival, while staff register the parcel and upload the photos. So the practical timeline is: purchase within 24 hours of payment, seller dispatch within days, then a 3–5 business day inspection window at the warehouse before photos are ready for your review. If you ordered several items from different sellers, consolidation waits on the slowest one.

Note that the 60-day free storage period starts when the order status changes to "Warehouse In" — the clock runs while you review photos and decide. Renewal is 10 RMB for another 30 days via the Renew button, and parcels unclaimed after 60 days are treated as abandoned and disposed of. Review QC photos promptly; a slow decision on one item can hold an entire consolidated parcel.

What to check in each photo: the five-minute review

Most agents' QC sets follow a similar pattern: overall shots, label/tag shots, and a couple of detail shots. Here is a universal checklist that works on any agent's photos. Budget about two minutes per item.

Color. Compare the QC photo against the listing image. Warehouse lighting is usually more neutral than professional product photography, so expect a small difference in tone — a deep red may look slightly flatter. But if you ordered dark green and the QC shows olive, or the listing showed navy and the photo shows royal blue, flag it. Color mix-ups are one of the most common seller errors.

Quantity and completeness. Does the parcel contain everything the listing promised? If the listing showed a set of three, are there three? Check for accessories, manuals, or bonus items shown in the listing. A missing charger or one absent earbud is far easier to resolve in China than after the parcel crosses the border.

Labels and tags. Read the size tag against what you ordered. If you ordered size 42, does the tag say 42? Check brand tags, wash labels, and model numbers where visible. This one simple check catches a surprising number of seller mix-ups — wrong size, wrong model, wrong variant.

Workmanship. Zoom in on every photo. On clothing and bags: check stitching lines for skipped or crooked stitches, loose threads, peeling edges, and glue residue. On electronics and accessories: check for scratches, dents, misaligned parts, and clean logo or text printing. Defects that look small in a photo can be very visible in person, so pinch-to-zoom on your phone and scan each image slowly.

Size and proportions. Does the item's shape match the listing? A jacket that looked structured in the listing but hangs limp in the QC photo is a quality red flag. Where the standard photos include measurements or a label, verify them against the listing's size chart — and note that you can request HD photos with measurement verification for items where fit decides the purchase.

Before you flag a minor difference, remember that listing photos are often professionally retouched. Judge the QC photo against the listing's stated specifications (size, color name, included items), not against the listing's styled photography.

The reject standard: when a defect actually gets flagged

Here is the part almost no buying-agent guide publishes: the threshold at which an agent marks an item as defective. CFans' stated defect standard is that visible defects of 0.5 cm or more in diameter, or 1 cm or more in length, get flagged during standard inspection.

That number matters because it defines the boundary of free QC. A scuff smaller than 0.5 cm may not be noted in the report at all — staff follow the standard, not your personal tolerance. So:

**Below the threshold (under 0.5 cm diameter / under 1 cm length)** — May pass inspection unflagged; decision falls on you; request HD close-ups if the detail matters to you

**At or above the threshold (0.5 cm+ diameter / 1 cm+ length)** — Flagged as a defect during standard inspection; you see it in the report and can decide to return or exchange

**Wrong item, wrong variant, wrong size, missing parts** — Not a judgment call; return or exchange regardless of size

If you are strict about quality — sneakers, branded accessories, visible hardware — do not rely on the standard threshold alone. Add a paid HD photo set (1.5 RMB per photo) and do your own inspection against your own standard. The free tier tells you the item broadly matches; the paid tier lets you verify it finely.

What happens when QC fails

Finding a problem is only useful if the return path is clear. According to CFans' Help Center terms:

- Eligible purchased goods have a 5-day no-questions-asked return and exchange service.
- Members are entitled to one free return or exchange within 30 days.
- After that, each returned item carries a handling fee of approximately $0.75 plus return shipping costs, totaling around $3 per item.

The key timing detail: act while the item is still at the warehouse. Once an item is consolidated into your international parcel and dispatched, it cannot be pulled back for a domestic return. Review photos as soon as they upload — do not let a questionable item sit while the 60-day storage clock and the consolidation of your other items move forward.

If the seller sent the wrong item or a defective one, your agent can handle the return communication in Chinese on your behalf — and clear QC images of the defect strengthen any return request.

The limits of QC: what photos cannot tell you

Honest QC marketing says what photos do not cover, so set your expectations accordingly:

**Authenticity.** QC photos show what the item looks like, not whether it is genuine. If you are buying branded goods, do not treat warehouse photos as authentication. A logo that is cleanly printed is not proof of a genuine product.

**Hidden quality.** Photos cannot show what happens under the surface — the durability of a zipper after a month, whether a battery holds a charge, whether fabric pills after washing. Standard inspection checks appearance details (style, color, size, model, damage, stains, defects), not engineering or longevity.

**Fit.** A size tag that matches your order does not mean the garment fits you. Cross-check the listing's measurement chart against your own body measurements before approving — ideally before ordering. (See the taobao-size-guide-2026 companion guide.)

**Packaging fidelity.** Goods are repackaged after inspection, and the original retail packaging may not be fully restored. If pristine original packaging matters (gifts, resell), confirm handling options with support before inspection happens.

QC is a risk-control checkpoint, not a guarantee. It catches the wrong color, the visible stain, and the missing accessory — the problems that cost the most to fix after international delivery. Everything else remains your judgment call.

When the paid upgrades are worth it

- Order HD custom photos (1.5 RMB/photo) when: the item costs enough that a flaw would hurt; the standard set missed a critical detail; you need measurement verification on fit-sensitive clothing.
- Order video QC (35 RMB/item) when: the item's quality is about shape, movement, or texture — a jacket's drape, a bag's structure, a shoe's flexibility; still photos keep coming back inconclusive.
- Skip both when: the item is inexpensive and simple; the standard 3–5 photos clearly show a correct, clean item; you would rather save the money for a better shipping line.

Frequently asked questions

Q: What are QC photos on a Taobao agent?
A: They are the quality-check photos warehouse staff take after your item arrives at the agent's China warehouse and before it ships internationally. You review them in your account and approve the item — or request a return — before paying for international shipping.

Q: Are QC photos free?
A: The standard inspection tier is the default flow with no separate fee stated, and it includes 3–5 photos per item (the count varies by product category). HD custom photos at 1.5 RMB per photo and video QC at 35 RMB per item are paid upgrades, both delivered within 24 hours.

Q: How many QC photos do you get per item?
A: Standard inspection provides 3–5 photos per item depending on the product category. If the standard set does not cover a detail you care about, you can request paid HD photos at 1.5 RMB per photo.

Q: What should I check in QC photos?
A: Five things: color accuracy against the listing, quantity and completeness of all parts, labels and size tags against your order, workmanship (stitching, glue residue, scratches, dents), and overall shape and proportions. Zoom in on every photo — defects that look small on screen can be very visible in person.

Q: What counts as a defect during standard QC?
A: CFans' published standard: visible defects of 0.5 cm or more in diameter, or 1 cm or more in length, get flagged. Smaller imperfections may pass unflagged, so request HD close-ups if you hold a stricter standard.

Q: How long does QC take?
A: Warehouse check-in QC takes 3–5 business days from domestic arrival at the warehouse, while staff register the parcel and upload photos. Add the seller's domestic shipping time before that, and the purchase itself is completed within 24 hours after your payment.

Q: What happens if an item fails QC?
A: Request a return or exchange while the item is still at the warehouse. CFans offers a 5-day no-questions-asked return and exchange service on eligible goods; members get one free return or exchange within 30 days, and subsequent returns cost approximately $0.75 in handling fees plus return shipping (around $3 per item total). Do not approve the item into your consolidated parcel if you intend to return it.

Q: Do QC photos verify that an item is authentic?
A: No. QC photos show appearance — color, style, labels, visible defects — not authenticity. They also do not guarantee hidden quality or durability. Treat them as a risk-control checkpoint, not an authentication service.

Q: Can I ask the warehouse to measure my item?
A: You can request measurement verification via paid HD custom photos (1.5 RMB per photo), which is the practical way to confirm sizing on clothing and shoes before approving shipment.
