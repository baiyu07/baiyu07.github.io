Step 0: Read the tracking — stuck, lost, or just waiting?

Most panic comes from misreading a tracking status — a parcel quietly moving between scan points can look "stuck" when it is fine. Diagnose first:

**Inbound into customs / Arrived at customs** — Status: the parcel has reached the destination country's border and is queued for clearance; Action: wait 3 business days, then check email/spam for a duty or document notice; this is normal processing, not a problem yet
**Held by customs / Awaiting clearance** — Status: customs needs something before releasing it (payment, document, or inspection); Action: check the carrier's official tracking page and your inbox for a specific request; act within days, not weeks
**Customs inspection / Goods detained for inspection** — Status: officers selected the parcel for a closer look; Action: usually resolves on its own in days; do not submit duplicate inquiries, but prepare documents in case asked
**Held at customs — incorrect paperwork** — Status: the declaration or documents do not match the contents; Action: contact your buying agent immediately — only the sender can supply corrected information to the carrier
**No update for weeks after "handed to airline / departed"** — Status: likely a transit gap (no scans during the flight or sea leg) or a missed scan at handoff; Action: wait; tracking often resumes at the destination sorting facility
**No update for a month or more with no customs scan at all** — Status: the parcel may be genuinely lost or stalled; Action: ask your buying agent to start a formal trace investigation with the carrier

Why parcels from China get held at customs

Customs holds fall into a handful of repeatable causes — knowing which one decides your next move.

**Unpaid duties or taxes** — The most common cause. The carrier or customs notifies the recipient; until the amount is settled, the parcel stays put. Note that duties are the recipient's responsibility in most cases, even when the seller collected shipping from you.
**Missing or incorrect paperwork** — The commercial invoice or declaration is incomplete, vague ("gift," "accessories" with no detail), or the declared value looks inconsistent with the contents. Parcels with low or false value declarations risk confiscation.
**Inspection selection** — Random or risk-based checks. Food, cosmetics, liquids, batteries, magnets, branded goods and high-value parcels are inspected more often.
**Restricted or prohibited items** — If the contents cannot legally enter the destination, the parcel is seized or returned — this is not a "delay" you can pay your way out of.
**Wrong or incomplete recipient details** — If customs or the carrier cannot reach you about a payment or document, nothing moves. Check that your order has your correct phone number and email.

One CBP rule is worth memorizing: when you buy from a foreign source, you become the importer, and the importer is responsible for import compliance — misrepresenting an item's nature or value on the declaration is illegal: https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/internet-purchases.

The 7-step rescue plan for a parcel stuck at customs

**1. Confirm the exact status on the carrier's official site.** Note the date of the latest scan. Look for a clear request — a payment button, a document upload link, or a named contact. Screenshot the status with the date; you will need it if you escalate later.

**2. Check your email and spam folder.** Carriers notify the recipient through the email or phone number on the order. Search for the carrier's name and your tracking number. Customs authorities almost never contact recipients directly — the message comes via the carrier or local postal service.

**3. Contact your buying agent, not just the seller.** This is the step generic guides get wrong for agent buyers. On the shipping label, your buying agent is the sender — and only the sender can correct declaration paperwork or open a formal trace with the carrier. If you ordered through CFans, open a support ticket with your order number and tracking number and describe the exact customs status. According to CFans, purchase-related support tickets are processed within 24 hours.

**4. Pay any duties through official channels only.** If a fee is due, pay it on the carrier's official website or at the post office — never through a link in an unexpected text message. Open the carrier's site yourself and confirm the same charge appears there.

**5. Submit requested documents promptly.** If customs wants proof of value or a product description, gather it before contacting anyone: order invoice, product listing screenshots, payment record, and the tracking number. When the agent submits corrected documents, follow up with the reference number and keep records of every submission.

**6. Follow up on a schedule.** A standard customs check often clears within a few business days once the issue is resolved. If the status has not moved for more than a week after you submitted everything, contact the carrier again and ask for the specific reason code — "awaiting documentation," "flagged for inspection," or "duty payment required" tell you very different stories.

**7. Know the last-resort options.** If the parcel cannot be released — for example, the contents are prohibited — your options are abandoning it, or asking the agent to arrange a return to China (you will usually bear the return freight and re-delivery fees). If the hold drags on with no explanation and the carrier cannot resolve it, escalate through your buying agent's support with your saved screenshots and ticket history.

Special case: stuck at China export customs

Sometimes the hold happens before the parcel even leaves China. If tracking shows the parcel sitting at an export customs point in China for over a week, it typically means the shipment needs a tax payment or a revised declaration on the China side. Only the sender — your buying agent or the carrier's China office — can handle this. Contact your agent and ask specifically whether the parcel needs export declaration documents or a duty payment, and ask for the reference number of their filing. Do not just wait silently; export holds do not always generate a recipient notification.

If your parcel is truly lost: trace, claim, reship or refund

A parcel is only "lost" after the formal process says so. Here is the path, in order.

**Start a trace through your agent.** CBP itself notes that when a package is lost, a tracer should be initiated by the sender of the package. You cannot open a carrier trace as the recipient in most cases. Give your agent the order number, tracking number, parcel description, and the date of the last scan. The carrier investigates and confirms whether the parcel is lost, misrouted, or sitting unscanned somewhere.

**File the insurance or carrier claim.** If you bought insurance when selecting your shipping line, this is when it pays. According to CFans, two insurance types are available at line selection — customs-seizure insurance and parcel loss/damage insurance, and some lines include insurance free. The payout equals actual shipping plus actual goods value, up to the insured amount. The claim window is 7 days after signing, or 45 days after shipping — file inside that window. Insurance prices are not published, so confirm the live price before you buy (verify live at checkout).

**Reship or refund.** Once the carrier confirms loss, the standard outcomes are a reshipment or a refund of the goods and shipping. Without insurance, recovery depends on the carrier's or agent's loss policy — ask your agent for the written terms before you assume anything. Keep every receipt: the order invoice, the shipping payment, the insurance purchase, and the trace reference number.

Watch out: customs-fee phishing texts

A special warning, because this scam targets exactly the buyers reading this article. You receive a text: "Your parcel is held at customs, pay $X now to release it," with a link. Real customs or carriers do not work this way. If you receive an unexpected payment message, do not tap its link — open the carrier's official website yourself, enter the tracking number, and check whether the same charge appears there. How to prevent this next time

**Declare accurately, every time.** According to CFans, buyers are advised to declare at least 30% of the total goods value, with a maximum of USD 135 (for Canada, the declared value must not exceed CAD 20). Under-declaring to dodge duties is the fastest way to get a parcel flagged, inspected, or seized — and the importer, meaning you, carries the liability.

**Give customs a reachable you.** Most "mystery holds" are just notifications that never reached the recipient — use contact details you actually check.

**Insure what you cannot afford to lose.** Insurance is cheapest at checkout and most valuable precisely in the scenario this article covers. Compare whether your line includes it free before paying extra.

Frequently asked questions

Q: How long does it take customs to clear a package?
A: Carrier guides commonly report clearance within 24 to 72 hours when documentation is complete and duties are paid; one to five business days is the range most often cited as normal. Inspection, missing paperwork, or unpaid fees can extend the hold to one or two weeks. If the status has not changed for more than a week, treat it as a problem and start the 7-step plan above.

Q: Will customs call me if they need something?
A: Rarely. Customs authorities almost never contact recipients directly — they communicate through the shipping carrier or local postal service, which emails or texts you about duty payments or missing information. Always check your spam folder, and verify any payment request on the carrier's official site before paying.

Q: What does "held at customs — incorrect paperwork" mean?
A: The declaration or supporting documents do not satisfy customs — vague descriptions, mismatched values, or missing invoices. Only the sender can correct it, so contact your buying agent with the exact status message and ask them to supply corrected documents to the carrier.

Q: Who should file a claim for a lost parcel — me or the seller?
A: The sender. Carriers require the shipper to initiate the trace and the claim. If you bought through a buying agent, the agent is the sender on the label — open a ticket with your order and tracking numbers and ask them to start the trace. Keep the trace reference number they give you.

Q: Can I get a refund if my parcel from China is lost?
A: Once the carrier confirms the loss, the usual outcomes are reshipment or a refund. If you bought parcel insurance, the claim follows the insurer's terms — with CFans, payout equals actual shipping plus actual goods value up to the insured amount, claimed within 7 days of signing or 45 days of shipping. Without insurance, check your agent's or carrier's written loss policy.

Q: My parcel has had no tracking update for a month. Is it lost?
A: Not necessarily. Long gaps happen during sea or air transit legs with no scanning points, and missed scans at carrier handoffs are common. If the last scan is a departure event and a month has passed with no destination scan, ask your buying agent to open a formal trace — that investigation, not the frozen tracking page, determines whether the parcel is lost.

Q: How can I avoid customs delays on my next order?
A: Declare accurately (CFans advises at least 30% of goods value, max USD 135), keep your contact details current so duty notices reach you, choose a shipping line that fits the goods, and consider insurance at checkout. Buying through one agent that handles purchase, QC, storage and shipping also gives you a single accountable contact if anything goes wrong.
