Before You Order: Vet the Seller, Not Just the Listing

On second-hand, the seller is the product information. Before sending any link to an agent:

  • Read the seller's history. Account age, transaction volume and past reviews say more than a low price does.
  • Demand real photos of the exact item. Stock images or manufacturer photos on a second-hand listing are a red flag. Ask for dated, specific shots — flaws included.
  • Test responsiveness. A seller who answers quickly and sends proof is signalling reliability. Silence or evasion is information too.
  • Never move payment off-platform. A seller who asks to transact outside the platform flow removes the only protection you have.
  • If a deal looks too good, it usually is. The rarest finds at the lowest prices are where mismatch risk concentrates.

Second-Hand Safety Checklist

  • Treat condition words as unverified. "Like new", "barely used" and "perfect" are subjective until QC photos prove them.
  • Check what's included. Boxes, cables, accessories and original parts are routinely missing on second-hand. Confirm before purchase.
  • Confirm after-sale terms for used items. Return and dispute coverage for second-hand differs from new goods — get it in writing before ordering.
  • Start cheap. One low-cost item through the full loop teaches you the seller-chat rhythm, QC quality and shipping time before anything expensive rides on it.

FAQ

Is Xianyu the same as Goofish?

Yes. Xianyu is the Chinese name of Alibaba's second-hand marketplace; Goofish is its international name. One marketplace, two names, same listings.

Do I need a Xianyu account to buy through an agent?

No. The agent holds the local account and address. You only need the listing link and an account with the agent.

Can I haggle on Xianyu through an agent?

Yes — the agent messages the seller in Chinese and can negotiate price or request extra photos before the purchase is made.

What if the item arrives different from the photos?

Raise it while the item is at the warehouse, where exchange or return is still practical. After international shipping, your options narrow sharply — which is why the QC step exists.
