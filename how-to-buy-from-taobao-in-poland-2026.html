Why Taobao Does Not Ship to Poland Directly

Taobao is a Chinese-language marketplace built for buyers inside China. Most sellers list a domestic delivery address as the only option. There is no Polish version of the site: the checkout, the seller’s messages and any dispute all happen in Chinese, and payment runs through Alipay, which often refuses a Polish card.

That is why the buying-agent model exists, and why it is the normal way Polish buyers order from Taobao. A buying agent is a company with a warehouse in China that buys on your behalf: you send the link, it places the order, the seller delivers to its warehouse, and the agent ships the parcel on to you. The agent also handles the Chinese-language parts: talking to the seller, checking the parcel, and dealing with a wrong or damaged item while a return inside China is still cheap.

The warehouse check is the part that matters most on a first order. Buy direct and nobody looks at the item before it crosses a border, so a wrong colour or a damaged item is discovered at your door in Poland — and sending it back to China can cost more than the item was worth. With an agent, the check happens in China, before the international leg is paid for. The same wall applies to 1688, only higher.

What You Will Pay at the Polish Border

This is the part of a first Taobao order that surprises Polish buyers most: the tax is a real part of the total cost, not a small extra, and it applies even to a cheap order. The rules below are the EU rules as they apply in Poland.

Polish VAT: 23% from the first euro

Poland’s standard VAT rate is 23% from the first euro — the old low-value exemption ended in July 2021. VAT is charged on the goods plus the shipping cost, so the freight you pay is part of the taxable amount. It can be paid in advance: the EU’s IOSS scheme lets VAT be prepaid at checkout on consignments worth €150 or less, so the parcel generally clears customs without a payment stop at delivery. Above €150, the standard import procedure applies instead. When you compare lines, ask whether the line is IOSS-enabled.

The €3 duty and the €2 handling fee: per distinct HS tariff line

Two further charges apply on low-value parcels, and the way they are counted is the detail that matters. Since 1 July 2026 the old €150 duty exemption no longer applies, and low-value B2C parcels pay a temporary €3 customs duty — a bridging measure that runs until July 2028, when standard duty rates resume. Since 1 November 2026 there is also a Union handling fee of €2. Both charges are counted per distinct HS tariff line: per product category on the customs declaration, not per piece and not per parcel. Three identical T-shirts are one tariff line, so that parcel pays €3 + €2 once — €5 in total. A T-shirt, a pair of jeans and a dress are three lines, so that parcel pays €3 + €2 three times — €15 in total. The number of pieces is not what counts; the number of different product categories is — the same counting Poczta Polska and the Polish tax administration use in their own notices. Unlike the €3 duty, the €2 fee has no value limit and is permanent: it applies to a parcel of any value and is not refunded on return.

How much VAT and duty will I pay on a Taobao order to Poland?

On top of VAT, low-value parcels pay a temporary €3 customs duty and a €2 Union handling fee, both per distinct HS tariff line — three identical T-shirts count as one line and pay €3 + €2 once, while a T-shirt, jeans and a dress count as three lines and pay €3 + €2 three times. What is IOSS, and do I need it for Poland?

IOSS is the EU scheme for prepaying import VAT at checkout on consignments worth €150 or less. You do not arrange it yourself — you need to know whether the shipping line you choose uses it. On an IOSS line the VAT is prepaid and the parcel generally clears customs without a payment stop at delivery; without IOSS the carrier collects the VAT, plus a handling fee, before delivery. Above €150, the standard import procedure applies instead.
