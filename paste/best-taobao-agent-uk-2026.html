Post-Brexit Customs and VAT: What UK Buyers Should Know

Since Brexit, the UK is outside the EU customs union, so parcels from China are treated as imports from a non-EU country. Here is what that means in practice — described procedurally, because rates and thresholds change and you should always check current UK government guidance:

  • Customs declarations are mandatory. Your agent completes a customs declaration for every parcel. Declare the contents honestly — under-declaring to dodge charges can get your parcel seized or destroyed.
  • Import VAT may apply. Goods imported into the UK may be subject to UK import VAT collected at or after delivery. The exact treatment depends on the consignment value and current HMRC rules, so check the official guidance before you order.
  • Choose a line with reliable customs handling. This is one of the biggest practical differences between agents. CFans' UK Royal Mail line is a dedicated UK route with commercial customs clearance built in, which means fewer surprises than a generic untracked postal option.
  • Keep your order value realistic. Splitting one giant haul into two sensibly-sized parcels is sometimes smarter than one enormous box — partly for customs simplicity, partly because volumetric weight pricing can punish oversized packaging.

None of this should put you off. Millions of parcels flow from China to the UK every month through exactly this system; you just need an agent whose UK line is built for it rather than an afterthought.
