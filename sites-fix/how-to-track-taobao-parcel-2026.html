Each leg can carry a different tracking number, and each number works in a different system. Why Taobao tracking confuses first-time buyers: the 3 tracking numbers

Most tracking frustration comes from checking the wrong number at the wrong stage. There are three distinct numbers in a typical agent-assisted order:

Tracking number 1 — the domestic leg — Seller: ships to the agent's warehouse inside China; Carrier: a Chinese domestic courier; Where it works: Chinese courier site, Cainiao, 17TRACK (domestic leg only)

Tracking number 2 — the international waybill — Agent: issues after consolidation and payment for international shipping; Carrier: the international line you selected; Where it works: agent dashboard, 17TRACK, Cainiao global, carrier site

Tracking number 3 — the last-mile handoff — Local courier: takes over after customs clearance in your country; Carrier: your national post or local courier; Where it works: your country's postal site, 17TRACK

The domestic number dies at the warehouse door. The international waybill is the number that matters for the long journey, and it only exists after you have paid for international shipping and the agent has handed the parcel to the carrier. The last-mile number appears only after customs clearance — before that, your home-country post has nothing to show.

Tracking number 1: the domestic leg (seller to warehouse)

When the seller ships your item, a Chinese domestic courier tracking number is generated. You can follow it while the parcel moves inside China, and its job ends the moment the parcel reaches the agent's warehouse.

Where to find it: in your CFans account, open the order and look at the order detail page. The domestic tracking number is listed alongside the seller shipment information, and its statuses update as the courier moves the parcel toward the warehouse.

What it tells you: whether the seller has actually shipped, where the parcel is inside China, and when it arrives at the warehouse. Once the status changes to warehouse arrival, the next checkpoint is intake inspection: according to CFans, warehouse check-in QC takes 3–5 business days from domestic arrival, during which staff register the parcel, inspect it, and upload 3–5 inspection photos. If the order shows "arrived" but photos are not up yet within that window, that is still normal processing — wait for the QC step to complete before worrying.

Tracking number 2: the international waybill (after consolidation)

This is the number most buyers are actually looking for, and it is also the one most people misunderstand. It is created only after your items have been checked, consolidated into one parcel, and handed to the international carrier. There is no international tracking number while your goods are still sitting in the warehouse awaiting your shipping instructions — the agent cannot generate a waybill for a parcel that does not exist yet.

Where to find it: in your CFans account, open the shipment or parcel record. The international waybill number appears once the parcel is dispatched. Copy it exactly — one wrong digit returns "not found" on every tracker.

The blackout period: expect no scan data for the first 24–48 hours after handover. The carrier must accept, scan, and batch the parcel before anything appears online, and a "not found" result during this window is normal. Note that some waybills only become trackable after the parcel clears your country's customs — before that point, trackers may show nothing at all even though the parcel is moving. Do not open a dispute during the blackout; wait for the first real scan.

Tracking number 3: the last-mile handoff number

After customs clearance in your country, the parcel is handed to a local courier or your national postal service, which assigns its own tracking number. The international waybill often keeps working on 17TRACK and Cainiao (they link the handoff), but your home post's website usually requires the local number.

Where to find it: check the international waybill on 17TRACK — the handoff number is frequently listed in the later tracking events. If it is not, ask your agent: they can see the handoff record on the Chinese side.

Reading the status map: what each status means and what to do

Order shipped / seller dispatched — Leg: domestic; Meaning: the seller handed the parcel to a Chinese courier; Action: watch the domestic number, nothing to do yet

Arrived at warehouse — Leg: domestic handoff; Meaning: the parcel reached the agent's warehouse; Action: wait for intake QC (3–5 business days), then review QC photos

QC passed / inspection complete — Leg: warehouse; Meaning: the item was checked and photographed; Action: review the photos, approve or request a return while the item is still in China

Consolidated / packed — Leg: warehouse; Meaning: your items were combined into one international parcel; Action: confirm the packed weight and pay for international shipping

Handed to carrier / linehaul departed — Leg: international start; Meaning: the international waybill is now live; Action: start tracking on 17TRACK; allow 24–48 hours for the first scan

Export customs clearance — Leg: China export; Meaning: the parcel is being processed for exit; Action: wait — short pauses here are routine

In transit / arrived at destination country — Leg: international; Meaning: the parcel reached your country and awaits import processing; Action: wait; check whether a local handoff number has appeared

Customs clearance in progress (destination) — Leg: destination customs; Meaning: your country's customs is reviewing the parcel; Action: see the customs section below; most clear without action

Handed to local carrier — Leg: last mile; Meaning: cleared customs, now with your national post or courier; Action: switch to the local tracking number if provided

Out for delivery / delivered — Leg: last mile; Meaning: final delivery in progress or complete; Action: if marked delivered but nothing arrived, check with neighbors and the local post office before escalating

Normal gaps vs red flags: the 10-day rule

Tracking goes quiet for normal reasons: the parcel is on a plane or ship (no scans mid-journey), it is sitting in a customs queue, or the carrier simply batches scans. Experienced buyers use a rough 10-day rule:

Under 10 days without an update — Normal. Keep watching. Cross-check on a second tracker (17TRACK plus Cainiao, or the carrier site) to rule out a feed gap.

10–14 days without an update — Time to ask. Contact your buying agent with the tracking number and order ID. The agent has direct channels to the Chinese carrier partner that you cannot access as an individual buyer, and can push for a status update or re-submit paperwork if customs needs it. Past the delivery window with no movement, or the carrier marks it returned — Escalate. Ask the agent about a formal trace or claim. If the parcel is confirmed lost, the insurance and claim rules below apply.

Customs statuses, decoded

"Customs clearance in progress" is the status that worries buyers most, and it is usually the least alarming. It means your country's customs authority is reviewing the parcel — checking the declaration, and sometimes assessing duties. Most parcels clear without any action from you.

When you might need to act: the courier contacts you (by text, email, or a message on the tracking page) asking for an ID number, a commercial invoice, or confirmation of the declared value. Courier websites often time out these requests after roughly 5–10 days — if you miss the window, the parcel can be returned to sender. So check the tracking page itself, not just the status line: the action request is usually written there.

Get the declaration right from the start to reduce customs friction. According to CFans' Help Center guidance, declare at least 30% of the total goods value, with a maximum declared value of USD 135 — and for shipments to Canada the declared value must not exceed CAD 20. An honest, accurate declaration is the simplest way to avoid a customs hold.

If the parcel is truly lost: insurance and claim windows

If the escalation path ends with a confirmed loss, two things matter: whether you bought insurance, and whether you act inside the claim window. According to CFans, two insurance types are available when you select the shipping line — customs-seizure insurance and parcel loss/damage insurance — and some lines include coverage free. Payout equals the actual shipping cost plus the actual goods value, up to the insured amount. Insurance prices are not published, so confirm the live price before paying (verify live).

Act inside the window: CFans' claim window is within 7 days of signing, or within 45 days of shipping. Keep screenshots of the full tracking history, your order record, and all correspondence with the agent and carrier — documentation decides claims.

Frequently asked questions

Q: Where can I find my Taobao tracking number?

A: There are three, at three stages. The domestic number is on the order detail page once the seller ships; the international waybill appears in your CFans shipment record after consolidation and dispatch; the last-mile number shows up on 17TRACK after customs clearance, or ask your agent for it.

Q: Why isn't my Taobao package moving in the tracking history?

A: The most common reasons are normal: the 24–48 hour blackout after carrier handover, a long-haul flight or sea leg with no intermediate scans, a customs queue, or one tracker polling a stale carrier feed. Cross-check on a second tracker first. Only treat it as a problem after about 10 days of no movement — then contact your agent with the tracking number and order ID.

Q: Can I track my Taobao parcel without a tracking number?

A: Only partially. Your CFans account shows order and warehouse checkpoints (purchased, arrived, QC photos, consolidated, dispatched) without any tracking number. But carrier-level tracking — where the parcel physically is — always requires the waybill number. If you cannot find it, check the shipment record in your account or ask support; do not guess digits.

Q: What does "customs clearance in progress" mean?

A: It means your country's customs authority is reviewing the parcel before release. Most parcels clear on their own within days. Check the tracking page for any action request (ID, invoice, or declaration confirmation) and respond within a few days — couriers can return parcels if requests time out.

Q: How long does it take for Taobao tracking to update after the agent ships my parcel?

A: Allow 24–48 hours for the first carrier scan to appear. Before that, "not found" results are normal and do not mean the number is wrong. Some waybills only become trackable after destination customs clearance, so an empty result early on is not proof of a problem.

Q: My tracking says "delivered" but I received nothing. What now?

A: First check with household members, neighbors, and your local post office — "delivered" scans are sometimes premature or the parcel is held for pickup. Get the last-mile tracking number from 17TRACK or your agent and ask the local carrier where exactly it was left. If it is genuinely missing, report it to your agent within the claim window (within 7 days of the delivery scan).
