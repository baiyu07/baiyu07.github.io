Xianyu vs Goofish: The Same Marketplace

If a seller link confuses you, this is the one fact to lock in: Xianyu (闲鱼, literally "idle fish") and Goofish are the same Alibaba marketplace. Xianyu is the Chinese name; Goofish is the name used for international users. A listing link from either name goes to the same item, and an agent that accepts one accepts the other. Guides that treat them as different platforms are describing one marketplace twice.

Why Overseas Buyers Need a Xianyu Agent

Everything that makes Xianyu attractive also makes it hard to buy directly:

  • Private sellers, not shops. Listings are created by individuals. Descriptions are personal, photos are phone snaps, and there is no store standard to fall back on.
  • China-only logistics. Sellers ship domestically, expect a Chinese address, and are paid through Chinese payment methods.
  • Chinese-only communication. Condition questions, haggling and delivery follow-ups all happen in Chinese — and condition questions are the entire purchase on second-hand.
  • Higher scam and mismatch risk. Second-hand means the item's true condition is unknowable from the listing alone. Overseas buyers have the least protection if it arrives wrong.

An agent solves the logistics and adds the one protection that matters: eyes on the item in China before it ships abroad. The agent contacts the seller, pays, receives the parcel at its warehouse, and photographs what actually turned up — so you approve the true condition, not the seller's description of it.

Second-Hand Safety Checklist

  • Read the seller, not just the listing. Account age, transaction history, response speed and willingness to send proof photos all signal more than a low price does.
  • Demand real photos of the exact item. Stock images or the manufacturer's photos on a second-hand listing are a red flag. Ask for dated, specific shots — flaws included.
  • Treat "like new" as unverified. On second-hand, condition words are subjective. The QC photo is the verification; the description is the claim.
  • Never pay outside the platform flow. A seller who asks to move payment off-platform removes the only protection you have.
  • Check second-hand after-sale coverage before you order. Agents' return and dispute terms for used items differ from new goods — confirm what happens if the item arrives materially different.
  • If a deal looks too good, it usually is. The rarest finds at the lowest prices are where mismatch risk concentrates.
